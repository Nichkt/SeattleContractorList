Review a contractor

Rate the work on six separate axes. Partial reviews are fine — skip anything you cannot speak to honestly, and it will be excluded rather than counted against them.

Please read this before rating. It is the whole point of the scale.

This site uses a deliberately tight scale, like Tabelog's. 3 means the contractor did what they promised — that is a good outcome and a good score, not a complaint. If every job gets five stars the number stops carrying any information, and the directory becomes useless to the next homeowner.

5
Exceptional. Genuinely better than the trade's norm. Rare by definition.
4
Clearly above expectations in a way you would mention to a friend.
3
Did the job properly, as agreed. A perfectly good result.
2
Real problems, but ultimately resolved or tolerable.
1
Serious failure — work you had to have redone or fight about.
Cancel

Write only about work you personally paid for and experienced. Reviews of named local businesses carry real consequences for real people — a fabricated or exaggerated review can materially damage a small contractor, and the scoring system is built specifically so that a handful of reviews cannot swing a score anyway. © .
