What the license does not tell you

The state's registration means a contractor filed paperwork, posted a bond, and pays for insurance. It says nothing about whether he can build a shower that holds water in five years. Nobody from the state watched him work. The credentials below are graded by people who did. Here is what each one means and where to check it.

Gold. Hands-on and hard to get. Years in the trade, then a real test.
Silver. Real training and a test, usually written.
Baseline. Required by law or paid for. A floor, not a distinction.

One caution. Many manufacturer programs, especially in roofing and siding, are partly a sales relationship. They do require a license, insurance, and volume. They also sell product. Look at the workmanship warranty they unlock. That is usually the part worth having.
