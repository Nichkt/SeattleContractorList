Before you sign anything

  1. Re-verify on L&I yourself. Every listing links straight to its official record. Registration and insurance can lapse between your page load and your contract signing, and the state's page is the authority — not this site.
  2. Know what the bond actually covers. Washington requires only a $12,000 bond for general contractors and $6,000 for specialty contractors. On a $90,000 remodel that is close to symbolic. A larger voluntary bond is a real signal.
  3. Ask for the certificate of insurance directly. Have the carrier send it to you naming your project. L&I shows a policy exists; it does not show whether your specific job is within its scope.
  4. Check the certifications, not just the license. Registration is a legal minimum — filing paperwork and posting a bond. It is not a test of skill. The trade certifications in our certification guide are.
  5. Look up the lawsuit and complaint history. L&I's detail page shows bond claims and infractions. It is the single most underused field on the page.
