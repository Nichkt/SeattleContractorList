Five things to do before you sign

  1. Check the state's record yourself. Every listing links to it. A license can lapse between the day you read this and the day you sign.
  2. Know how small the bond is. The state asks $30,000 from a general contractor and $15,000 from a specialty contractor, raised on July 1, 2024 from $12,000 and $6,000. On a $90,000 kitchen that does not go far, and if you file a claim other homeowners may be ahead of you in line. A contractor who posts more than the law requires is worth a second look.
  3. Get the insurance certificate from the carrier. Ask them to name your job on it. The state can tell you a policy exists. It cannot tell you your work is covered.
  4. A license is not a test of skill. It means the paperwork is filed and the bond is posted. Nobody from the state watched the work. The credentials in our certification guide are graded by people who did.
  5. Read the complaint history. L&I lists bond claims and infractions on its own pages. Most people skip it.

Questions people ask

How many contractors are registered in King County?

17,122 businesses hold an active Washington contractor registration with a King County address. A further 2,237 are currently suspended and 13,806 have expired — they still turn up in search results, so check before you call.

What does this site do that the state site does not?

The state lets you look up one contractor at a time, if you already know who you are looking for. This joins the registry to the bond file and the insurance file and lets you browse — by trade, by city, sorted, filtered. Same records, same source, arranged so you can start from "I need a roofer" instead of from a licence number.

How much is a contractor bond in Washington?

$30,000 for a general contractor and $15,000 for a specialty contractor, since July 1, 2024. Before that they were $12,000 and $6,000 and had not moved in twenty-two years. See what that covers on your job with the bond calculator.

Does a listing here mean you recommend them?

No. It means the paperwork is in order. Nobody pays to be listed, nobody is promoted, and no contractor can buy a position. That is the whole arrangement.

How current is this?

The pages are rebuilt from the state's files every week — this one on Tue Aug 18 2026 — and the live lookup re-checks against L&I when you open a page. A registration can still lapse between your reading and your signing, which is why every listing links to the state's own record.
