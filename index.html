Five things to do before you sign

  1. Check the state's record yourself. Every listing links to it. A license can lapse between the day you read this and the day you sign.
  2. Know how small the bond is. The state asks $12,000 from a general contractor and $6,000 from a specialty contractor. On a $90,000 kitchen that does not go far. If you file a claim, other homeowners may be ahead of you in line. A contractor who posts more than the law requires is worth a second look.
  3. Get the insurance certificate from the carrier. Ask them to name your job on it. The state can tell you a policy exists. It cannot tell you your work is covered.
  4. A license is not a test of skill. It means the paperwork is filed and the bond is posted. Nobody from the state watched the work. The credentials in our certification guide are graded by people who did.
  5. Read the complaint history. L&I lists bond claims and infractions on the same page. Most people skip it.

We are not the government and do not speak for it. This is an independent site built on public records. It is not affiliated with Washington State L&I or King County. Every listing comes straight from the state registry. A listing here is not a recommendation. It means the paperwork is in order.
