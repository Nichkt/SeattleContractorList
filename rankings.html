Rankings & awards

Ranked by weighted score, per trade. The scale is deliberately hard to climb: most contractors land between 3.0 and 3.5, and a 3.5 is genuinely good. Awards are earned against the whole rated population — Gold is the top 1%, Silver the top 5%, Bronze the top 15% — rather than handed to everyone who clears a fixed number.

Why the numbers look low

Because a scale where everyone scores 4.7 tells you nothing. Two mechanisms keep this one informative:

  1. Scores are pulled toward the average until they are earned. One five-star review moves a contractor to about 3.46, not 5.00. Reaching 4.0 takes both excellence and volume — which is also why a handful of reviews from friends cannot manufacture a ranking.
  2. Ratings are recalibrated against the whole population. Home-services reviews run generous — people rate a contractor they met and shook hands with far above what they would give a restaurant. The scale is remapped so the typical King County contractor sits near 3.2, which restores the meaning of the numbers above it.
  3. Old reviews fade. Weight halves every four years, because a review from 2019 may describe a company that no longer employs any of the same people.
  4. Provisional businesses are never ranked or awarded. Below three reviews, a score is shown but explicitly marked unreliable and excluded from every list on this page.

Rankings reflect only reviews submitted to this site, which is a far smaller sample than the major platforms. A contractor absent from these lists is almost certainly just unreviewed here — not judged and found wanting. © .
