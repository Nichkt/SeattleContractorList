The state of contracting in King County, 2026

Washington publishes its contractor registry as open data and then leaves it alone. Nobody adds it up. This is the county's ledger for 2026: who is registered, who is covered, and what the record does not tell you.

Built Tue Aug 18 2026 from data.wa.gov.

The headline

17,122 businesses hold an active Washington contractor registration with a King County address. Another 2,237 are suspended right now, 13,806 have let a registration expire, and 997 are recorded as out of business. The county's full file runs to 36,242 records, so a name turning up in a web search proves very little on its own.

The number worth sitting with is 2,237. That is 11.6% of everyone who holds or recently held an active registration here. A suspended contractor's website looks exactly like anyone else's. They are still advertising. They still answer the phone. The state has simply told them to stop working, and there is nothing on the outside of the business that says so.

The trades

General contractors dominate: 11,751 registrations, or 69% of the county. After them it drops off sharply: electrical at 1,081, painting & wallcovering at 671, flooring & countertops at 562, landscaping & tree care at 534, and plumbing at 449. The long tail is genuinely thin. 6 of the twenty-seven trades we track have fewer than fifty registered businesses in the whole county, which is worth remembering the next time somebody tells you to get three quotes.

Where they are

Seattle accounts for 3,775 registrations, 22% of the county. After it, Auburn (1,612), Kent (1,515), Renton (1,267), Federal Way (1,041). Bear in mind the state records a mailing address and not a service area. A contractor registered in Kent will drive to Sammamish, and the address tells you where the paperwork goes rather than where the truck turns up.

Bonds

July 1, 2024 was the first change to Washington's bond amounts in twenty-two years. A general contractor's bond went from $12,000 to $30,000, and a specialty contractor's from $6,000 to $15,000. The old numbers were set in 2002 and had spent two decades quietly shrinking against the cost of the work they were meant to cover.

Among King County registrations with a bond on file, the median is $30,000 and the middle half runs $15,000 to $30,000. 120 — 2% of those with a published bond — carry more than the law requires. That is a real signal and it is free to look up. A contractor who posts triple the minimum has told a surety company something about themselves, and the surety agreed.

Even the new figure is small against the work. $30,000 covers a third of a $90,000 kitchen, and it is shared: several claimants against one bond line up and take a share. Bonded is a floor. It was never a guarantee, and the raised amounts have not made it one.

Insurance

99.8% of the county's construction and plumbing registrations carry a current liability policy on file — 15,992 of 16,021. That leaves roughly 29 actively registered businesses with no current policy published against their licence. It is the single most checkable thing on this page and it takes about fifteen seconds.

Claims

26 King County contractors have a bond recorded as impaired, which is the state's way of saying a claim was made and the surety paid. It is a small number and it is buried in a file nobody reads. Where we have it, it appears on the contractor's listing, because a paid claim is the closest thing in the public record to a verdict on somebody's work.

What the record cannot tell you

Washington publishes no open dataset of contractor infractions, complaints, or lawsuits. That history exists only on the state's own verification pages, one contractor at a time. Anyone claiming to rank Washington contractors by complaint volume is not working from published data, because there is none to work from.

The bond file is incomplete in a way that matters. 56% of active King County registrations have no bond row at all, and that share holds between 40% and 48% for every registration cohort back to 2010. A real compliance problem would vary by year. A flat line across sixteen years is a property of the file. Add that the law permits an assigned savings account instead of a surety bond, and the honest statement is narrow: the state publishes a current bond for 7,551 of 17,122 active King County registrations. What the rest have, the published data does not say.

And registration is not competence. It means fees were paid, a bond was posted, and a form was filed. Nobody from the state watched anyone build anything. It is the floor you should refuse to go below, and it is not a recommendation.

How this was built

Three files: the L&I contractor registry, the bond file, and the insurance file. They are joined on the contractor licence number and filtered to King County mailing addresses across 45 cities and towns. A bond or policy counts as current when its effective date has passed and no cancellation or expiry date has. Where a business has several bonds on file, the largest current one is used.

One trap is worth naming for anyone repeating the exercise. Socrata does not guarantee row order, so paging through these files without an explicit sort silently returns some rows twice and skips others. Pulled unordered, the bond file gave 80,000 rows holding 31,171 distinct licence numbers. Sorted, the same 80,000 rows held 37,508 — which is what the server's own distinct count reports. A sixth of the file, gone, with no error raised.

Questions people ask

May I quote these figures?

Yes. They come from public records and they are free to use. A link back lets your readers check the working, which is the whole point. Want a cut that is not here? A single city, a single trade, a longer run of years? The source files are linked at the foot of the page, and the method is described above.

How often is this updated?

The figures behind it are rebuilt from the state's files every week. The report carries the date it was built. Numbers quoted from an older edition will not match a newer one, so cite the date.

What would you not conclude from this?

That the county is full of contractors working without a bond. The published file is missing rows for reasons the data does not explain, and the law permits an assigned savings account in place of a surety bond. What we can say is narrow: the state publishes a current bond for 7,551 of 17,122 active registrations. Turning the remainder into a headline count would be inventing a finding the evidence does not carry.
